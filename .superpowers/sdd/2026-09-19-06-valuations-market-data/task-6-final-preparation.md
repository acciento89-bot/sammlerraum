## Narrow clarification: ambiguous transaction acknowledgement

Root context rulings 3/6 and reconciliation's distinction between safe spacing and failed operational cleanup support the following precise wording: **uncertainty latches a local lifecycle failure; a durable database claim remains blocking only if that claim is still present.** Do not claim every cleanup error guarantees globally durable BLOCKED state.

* **Admission COMMIT acknowledgement lost:** the owner must not invoke HTTP. If admission committed, its non-expiring claim and charged counters remain authoritative and another replica cannot admit. If admission rolled back, no claim was created; the uncertain owner still must not send or claim clean shutdown. No speculative cleanup/takeover or budget refund resolves the ambiguity.
* **Post-settlement compare-clear COMMIT acknowledgement lost:** complete physical transport settlement and the full monotonic cooldown have already occurred. If clear committed, the database can legitimately be idle and another replica can admit safely; if it did not commit, the claim remains blocking. The old owner latches cleanup uncertainty and later `stop()` rejects even after its handler settles. Existing worker shutdown therefore withholds database disconnect. This local failure does not manufacture a global blocked row, prove fleet drain, or authorize recovery/purge.

Future tests must distinguish actual database outcomes with a separate client: (1) force admission commit then simulate lost ACK, assert zero HTTP starts and successor blocked by the held claim; (2) complete transport/cooldown, force compare-clear commit then simulate lost ACK, assert database idle and successor may send only after that prior settlement/cooldown, while old lifecycle stop rejects; (3) fail clear before commit, assert claim remains held and local stop rejects. No test should require a blocking row after a successfully committed clear merely because its acknowledgement was lost.
